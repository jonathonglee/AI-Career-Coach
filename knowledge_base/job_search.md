# Comparing job descriptions with resume evidence

Identify required skills, preferred skills, duties, experience, education, location, and work arrangement. Create an evidence map: requirement, supporting resume example, and any gap. Use “not demonstrated” when the resume does not mention a skill; absence of evidence is not proof of absence. Prioritize essential requirements over optional ones. Suggest honest coursework, practice, or projects to strengthen evidence, and avoid making predictions about hiring outcomes.

# Career exploration for students

Explore occupations by daily tasks, skills, work settings, learning pathways, and personal interests. Coursework, part-time work, volunteering, clubs, and independent projects may reveal transferable skills. Compare several actual job descriptions before choosing a specialization. Informational interviews can clarify what work is like. Career titles vary by employer, so compare responsibilities rather than relying on titles alone.

# Organizing applications and follow-ups

Track each application with company, role, source URL, submission date, resume version, contacts, interview stages, deadlines, and next action. Save the job description because postings may disappear. Customize a truthful resume and short cover letter when appropriate. Follow employer instructions and keep a record of communications. Avoid submitting personal identity documents or payment information through suspicious recruiting messages.

# Evaluating internships and entry-level opportunities

Assess the actual duties, supervision, training, expected skills, schedule, compensation, location, and potential learning value. An internship can be valuable when it provides meaningful work and feedback; an entry-level job may still require practical experience. Consider commute, remote or hybrid requirements, and financial constraints. Verify eligibility and employment terms with the employer. Do not assume an internship guarantees a permanent job.

---

# Recognizing job-search scams and checking sources

Confirm openings through an employer’s official careers page or a trusted recruiting channel. Be cautious of requests for upfront payments, gift cards, bank credentials, or sensitive identity information before a legitimate onboarding process. Check sender domains and independently verify unexpected offers. Treat salary figures, job availability, and deadlines as time-sensitive and verify them from current sources. The knowledge base does not provide live job listings.
