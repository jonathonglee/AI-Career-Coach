# What applicant tracking systems do

Applicant tracking systems (ATS) help employers organize applications and may support searching, filtering, or screening. Features and configurations vary widely by employer and vendor. Do not claim there is one universal ATS score or a guaranteed method to pass automated screening. Human reviewers, application questions, and other processes may also affect decisions. Follow the employer’s instructions first.

---

# Readable resume formatting for parsing

Use standard section headings, readable fonts, consistent dates, and a straightforward text-based structure. Complex tables, text boxes, headers, footers, and decorative graphics may be harder for some systems to interpret, although behavior varies. Submit the file type requested by the employer and inspect the document after export. Plain formatting improves clarity but cannot guarantee successful parsing or selection.

---

# Honest keyword alignment

Identify important skills and terms from the posting and use them naturally when they accurately describe the applicant’s experience. Prefer evidence-based bullets that show how a tool was used. Do not hide keywords, copy the full posting, invent credentials, or list unfamiliar tools. Distinguish required from preferred qualifications. Tailoring helps communicate relevance but does not guarantee an interview.

---

# Checking applications and accessibility

Before submission, verify contact details, links, dates, spelling, and that text can be selected in the document. Check that the application form correctly imported employment and education information; correct errors manually. Keep a copy of the submitted resume and posting. Avoid disclosing sensitive personal data beyond what the legitimate application requires. Accessibility and readability benefit human reviewers as well as automated systems.
