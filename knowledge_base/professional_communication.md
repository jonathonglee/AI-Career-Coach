# Professional outreach to recruiters and alumni

Write a concise subject line and introduce yourself with a truthful connection or reason for contacting the person. Make a specific, reasonable request, such as a brief informational conversation or clarification about a role. Mention one relevant interest or experience and provide an easy way to respond. Avoid mass messages that falsely imply familiarity. Respect nonresponses and privacy.

---

# Cover letters and statements of interest

A focused cover letter connects the role’s needs with two or three concrete examples from the applicant’s background. Explain why the organization or work is interesting using verified information. Avoid repeating the resume word for word or making unsupported claims about the employer. Use a professional tone, proofread, and follow length or submission instructions. A cover letter should not invent accomplishments.

---

# Interview thank-you messages

A brief thank-you email can express appreciation, refer to a genuine conversation topic, and reiterate interest. Send it within a reasonable period after the interview when appropriate. Keep the message specific and professional; do not fabricate details or pressure the interviewer for an immediate decision. If multiple interviewers participated, personalize messages when practical.

---

# Application follow-up and status requests

Use the employer’s stated timeline when deciding when to follow up. If no timeline was given, wait a reasonable interval and send a short message identifying the position, date of application or interview, and a polite request for an update. Avoid repeated messages over a short period. If an offer has a deadline, communicate that clearly and respectfully rather than creating false urgency.

---

# Team communication and professional etiquette

In projects and workplaces, communicate progress with a clear status, completed work, blockers, and next steps. Make requests actionable and specify deadlines when necessary. Document decisions and distinguish facts from assumptions. Give credit for team contributions and resolve disagreements respectfully. Protect confidential information and avoid sending resumes or personal data to unintended recipients.
