# Behavioral interviews and the STAR method

Prepare honest examples using Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Describe the context briefly, explain your own responsibility, emphasize actions you personally took, and state an observable result or lesson. Practice examples involving teamwork, disagreement, time management, adaptability, customer service, and mistakes. A class project, volunteer activity, or part-time job can provide valid examples. Do not fabricate experiences or metrics.

---

# Technical interview preparation

Use the actual job posting to identify technical topics worth reviewing. For analytics roles, practice explaining SQL joins and aggregation, spreadsheet calculations, data cleaning, dashboards, and basic statistics when relevant. Explain project decisions: the question, data, method, tradeoffs, validation, findings, and limitations. When unsure, state what you know, describe how you would investigate, and avoid bluffing. Some roles use practical exercises; rehearse with representative tasks.

---

# Explaining projects clearly

Use a concise project narrative: business problem, intended audience, data source, your contribution, approach, challenge, outcome, and what you would improve. Be prepared to distinguish team contributions from your own work. Describe why a tool or visualization was chosen, not only how it was used. If a project used synthetic or sample data, disclose that fact. Do not imply that a class project was a production deployment unless it was.

---

# Researching the employer and asking questions

Before an interview, review the employer’s official website, role description, products or services, and recent publicly available information. Prepare thoughtful questions about day-to-day responsibilities, onboarding, team collaboration, success measures, and development opportunities. Avoid asking questions that are already clearly answered in the posting unless you need clarification. Evaluate the role and employer as well as preparing to be evaluated.

---

# Interview logistics and follow-up

Confirm the interview time, time zone, location or video link, format, and interviewer details. Test equipment, prepare a quiet setting, and bring appropriate materials. Answer concisely and ask for clarification when needed. Afterward, a short thank-you message can reference a genuine topic discussed and reiterate interest. Follow the employer’s stated timeline; if none is given, a polite status inquiry after a reasonable interval may be appropriate.
