# Resume structure and readability

Use clear headings such as Education, Experience, Projects, Leadership, and Skills. Put the most relevant evidence near the top and use reverse chronological order within sections. Keep dates, locations, verb tenses, and punctuation consistent. For most students and recent graduates, a focused one-page resume is a practical starting point, although longer resumes may be appropriate when relevant experience warrants it. Avoid dense paragraphs, decorative graphics, and confusing columns. Proofread carefully and use a readable, text-based layout.

---

# Writing evidence-based accomplishment bullets

Begin bullets with specific action verbs and describe what you did, how you did it, and the outcome or purpose. Example: “Built a Tableau dashboard from retail transaction data to compare monthly sales trends and presented findings to a class team.” Quantify scope or results only when the student can verify the numbers. Distinguish an outcome from an aspiration; do not claim business impact that was never measured. Avoid vague phrases such as “responsible for various tasks” when a concrete action is available.

---

# Tailoring a resume to a job description

Read the posting for required qualifications, preferred qualifications, duties, tools, and domain knowledge. Match each important requirement to evidence from coursework, employment, internships, or projects. Use accurate terminology from the posting where it reflects real experience. Prioritize relevant accomplishments instead of stuffing keywords. If a required skill is not mentioned, label it “not demonstrated on this resume” rather than assuming the applicant lacks it. Never invent credentials or work history.

---

# Presenting student projects and coursework

Academic and personal projects can demonstrate professional skills when paid experience is limited. For each project, include the problem, dataset or users, tools, individual contribution, deliverable, and any supported result. Distinguish team achievements from personal responsibilities. Relevant coursework can support a skills claim, but an applied example is stronger. Include a portfolio or GitHub link when it is accessible, professional, and safe to share; remove secrets and private data.

---

# Skills, education, and experience accuracy

Group skills by meaningful categories such as Analysis, Programming, Visualization, and Collaboration. List tools only when the student can discuss their use. State degree names, schools, dates, certifications, and job titles accurately. Use “Expected” for anticipated graduation dates. Explain employment gaps only when relevant; avoid unnecessary personal information. Do not include sensitive identifiers or references’ contact details unless specifically requested through an appropriate channel.
